- **[search summary]**: the page refused a direct fetch (403, captcha or 404) and the claim comes from the search engine's summary of it. Treat these as one step weaker.

## Q1. Godzilla (2014) main title sequence: redacted documents, 1950s nuclear-test archive, credits revealed through redaction

### Takeaway
The sequence was made by Prologue Films (Kyle Cooper, main title designer; Andrew Young, editor and co-producer). It frames real 1950s Pacific nuclear-test footage as a cover story: the "tests" were attempts to kill the monster. Cast and crew names appear inside "classified" Monarch and military documents while a virtual black marker strikes out the surrounding text. Designer interviews about it are thin. Art of the Title lists it as "title only" with no interview, so most craft detail comes from reviews and fan transcriptions.

### Cited Findings
- **Credits.** Main Title Designer Kyle Cooper; Main Title Producer Unjoo Byars; Main Title Editor/Co-Producer Andrew Young; Design & Animation David Badounts, Gary Mau, Matthew Normand, Ana Criado Zahonero; Production Coordinator Alexander Chen; studio Prologue Films; music Alexandre Desplat. Art of the Title tags its styles as "archive footage, archive photography, editorial, main title, mixed-media, prologue". The aspect ratio is 2.35:1 and the film was released May 16, 2014 — [Art of the Title: Godzilla (2014)](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/godzilla/) [fetched]
- Art of the Title has only the credit page for Godzilla (2014), marked "title only", while some other Cooper entries are marked "interview" — [Art of the Title: Kyle Cooper titles](https://www.artofthetitle.com/designer/kyle-cooper/titles/) [search summary]
- A Vimeo upload of the main title credits Prologue Films, with Kyle Cooper directing and Andrew Young editing — [Vimeo: Godzilla 2014 – Main Title](https://vimeo.com/176791526) [search summary]
- **Redaction mechanism.** The credits give "a brief visual history of the monster's rampages through history". The text around each name is quickly covered by a "virtual black marker" — [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/comicbook/news/godzilla-opening-credits-what-was-blacked-out/) [fetched]
- **Hidden jokes under the bars.** One uncovered line reads "Are these animals real? Can we prove they even exist?" Another mocks "men in rubber suits with costumes designed by tricksters". Bryan Cranston's credit document referenced *Breaking Bad* and *Malcolm in the Middle* before it was scrubbed, and "Walter White" flashes on screen for a split second after the censoring begins — [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/comicbook/news/godzilla-opening-credits-what-was-blacked-out/) [fetched]; [Slashfilm](https://slashfilm.com/walter-white-breaking-bad-godzilla-easter-egg) [search summary]
- The credits "use redacted military reports" to fill in the creature's history. The text mentions the "Bikini Islands", which ties it to Operation Crossroads. Badass Digest transcribed the full credits — [CinemaBlend](https://www.cinemablend.com/new/Godzilla-Easter-Eggs-Reference-Mothra-Naval-History-Breaking-Bad-43106-p2.html) [search summary; the fetched page came back truncated]
- Boing Boing's review describes "censored" white-outs placed around cast and crew names — [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2014/05/17/godzilla-2014.html) [search summary]. Note the conflict: Boing Boing says white, ComicBook.com says black marker. Only frame inspection can settle it, and the sequence may use both.
- **Story function.** The sequence shows the government trying to destroy Godzilla with nuclear weapons. Its "fast pace", archival-style footage and Desplat's "ominous music" set the stage. Screen Rant also reports, from a single secondhand account of Gareth Edwards at a Legendary watch party, that the sequence was originally planned as a scene — [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/godzilla-2014-opening-scene-filmed/) [search summary; direct fetch failed]
- The IMDb synopsis states that the 1950s tests were attempts to kill the creature, and Serizawa explains this later in the film — [IMDb synopsis](https://m.imdb.com/title/tt0831387/synopsis) [search summary]
- **Ending, per a fan blog (low authority).** "Godzilla rises up out of the ocean and is hit with the warhead, followed by stock footage of an actual atom bomb explosion and the shockwave that follows it." — [Cody's Film, TV, and Video Game Blog](http://codysfilmandtvblog.blogspot.com/2019/05/franchises-monsterverse-godzilla-2014.html) [search summary]
- Desplat's soundtrack album has a main cue titled "Godzilla!". No analysis of the cue was found — [Wikipedia: Godzilla (2014 film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godzilla_(2014_film)) [search summary]

### Inferences
- **The core device is a "documents that know more than you" layer over "footage you think you know."** Audiences recognise 1950s H-bomb newsreel. The redacted dossier recontextualises it as a cover-up, so the dread comes from reinterpreting real history, not from new imagery. This transfers directly to 1945–75 Vietnam material: cables, intelligence summaries and Pentagon Papers-style pages over familiar newsreel.
- **Redaction drives both rhythm and type.** Each card is a mini-beat: the document appears, the eye starts reading, the bars strike through, and only the credit survives. The strike-out gives a percussive cut point without needing a picture cut. That makes it a reproducible typographic beat for an automated system: reveal, read window, strike, hold name.
- **Hidden jokes and single-frame flashes like "Walter White"** reward pause-frame viewers. For a documentary, the equivalent is real but unhighlighted detail in the documents (dates, unit names) that holds up to pausing. Fabricated text should not be used.
- **Ending (recollection, not verified here).** The sequence climaxes on a countdown to the detonation and lets the atomic flash white out the frame before the film proper. The fan-blog quote above supports "ends on a real atomic explosion and shockwave". The countdown and white-out details need frame verification.

### Gaps
- No primary interview with Kyle Cooper or Andrew Young about this sequence was found. Art of the Title has no interview, and prologue.com pages returned 403.
- No shot count, runtime or cut-rhythm data. Runtime was not confirmed by any source fetched.
- No analysis of how Desplat's "Godzilla!" cue is structured against the picture (ostinato or brass hits on the strike-outs?). This needs listening and frame inspection.
- No full transcription of the redacted text was read. The Badass Digest transcription is cited by CinemaBlend but was not located, and Bloody Disgusting's "Redacted credits uncovered" page returned 403.

## Q2. MonsterVerse companions: Kong: Skull Island (2017), Godzilla: King of the Monsters (2019), Godzilla vs. Kong (2021), Monarch: Legacy of Monsters (2023)

### Takeaway
Prologue/Kyle Cooper extended the Godzilla 2014 grammar across the franchise. Kong: Skull Island's main title is a montage of 1940s–70s news footage plus a "Monarch film", backed by a long list of real archive licensors. Godzilla vs. Kong reuses the redacted-Monarch-records device and ends on a zoom into the Earth. Monarch's TV titles (Shine) swap redaction for a split screen that pits past against present.

### Cited Findings
- **Kong: Skull Island.** The opening is described as a montage of 1940s–70s news videos, with logos set in the air and "made of metal" to echo the aerial dogfight prologue — [IMDb crazy credits, Kong: Skull Island](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt3731562/crazycredits/) [search summary]
- **Kong: Skull Island credits.** "Main Title and Monarch Film" by Prologue/Kyle Cooper; title producer Kate Berry; title editors Shawn Fedorchuk and Rachel Fowler — [JH Movie Collection wiki: Kong: Skull Island/Credits](https://jhmoviecollection.fandom.com/wiki/Kong:_Skull_Island/Credits) [search summary]
- **Kong: Skull Island archive licensors** named in the end credits: Getty Images/Keystone, US Library of Congress Prints and Photographs Division, CNN, Thinkstock, Raymond Depardon/Magnum Photos, NOAA Photo Library, GoPro, Reda Archives, British Pathé — [SciFi Japan: Kong: Skull Island final credits](https://www.scifijapan.com/kaiju-monsters/kong-skull-island-final-credits) and the JH wiki above [search summary; the summary did not say clearly which page lists which licensor]
- Art of the Title's Cooper index lists Kong: Skull Island as "title only", with no interview — [Art of the Title: Kyle Cooper](https://www.artofthetitle.com/designer/kyle-cooper) [search summary]
- **Godzilla vs. Kong.** The opening is described as a montage of Monarch records and monitors showing Godzilla and Kong battles. All text on the documents is blacked out except the cast and crew names. The montage ends with a zoom into the Earth, from which the title appears — [IMDb crazy credits, tt5034838](https://imdb.de/title/tt5034838/crazycredits?tab=cz) [search summary; the summary flagged that this page might be mixing material between films]
- A search summary said an IMDb credits listing shows Prologue on the "2021 Godzilla vs. Kong" main titles, and that it found nothing tying Cooper to the King of the Monsters titles. But the listing it linked is IMDb ID tt3741700, which I believe is *King of the Monsters* (2019), not Godzilla vs. Kong (tt5034838). The summary has probably confused the two films — [IMDb reference tt3741700](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt3741700/reference); [Box Office Mojo credits tt3741700](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt3741700/credits) [search summary]. Screen Rant states Cooper did the King of the Monsters titles — [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/godzilla-2014-opening-scene-filmed/) [search summary]. Net: Prologue/Cooper on King of the Monsters is likely but not verified here, and Prologue on Godzilla vs. Kong is unverified.
- **Monarch: Legacy of Monsters (Shine).** "Shine designed and produced the main title sequence for Apple TV+ and Legendary's Monarch: Legacy of Monsters." It "uses a bold graphic split-screen treatment, dividing the frame down the center": "1952 against 2015, San Francisco against Tokyo, analog technology against digital technology, and past against present." "These visual pairings create a fast-moving montage that reflects the parallel timelines, locations, and generations." "Designed to intrigue without revealing too much, the imagery takes on new meaning after watching the first season." — [Shine: Monarch](https://www.shinestudio.com/project-pages/broadcast/monarch-legacy-of-monsters) [fetched]
- **Monarch materials and devices.** "Media imagery was used to show the parallel stories"; "Family photos helped tease the mystery"; "Temple Grandin's original drafting drawings"; "The migratory patterns of monsters was a graphic device"; "Light and shadow was a visual theme"; "Mirrored and echoed imagery sets up the theme". For season 2, "Sound waves were a visual that teased the reveal of the monster" — [Shine: Monarch](https://www.shinestudio.com/project-pages/broadcast/monarch-legacy-of-monsters) [fetched]. The same page says both "between 1952 and 2016" and "1952 against 2015", an internal inconsistency.
- A RISD alumni page credits Michael Riley (Shine) with designing and producing the Monarch titles — [RISD alumni](https://alumni.risd.edu/news-events/riley-michael-0) [search summary]

### Inferences
- **Franchise grammar.** (a) Real newsreel establishes "this happened". (b) A fictional agency's files reinterpret it. (c) Redaction or graphic devices carry the credits. (d) The montage ends on the scale shot that converts to the title: an atomic flash in 2014, a zoom into the Earth in GvK.
- **Monarch's split screen is a cheap, strong device for archive-only work.** Pair 1945 with 1975, or North with South, the same place then and now, or analogue radio with a later broadcast. Two archival clips side by side create meaning without narration. The "mirrored and echoed imagery" principle means the pairs should rhyme in composition (same screen direction or shape), not just in subject.
- **"Migratory patterns as a graphic device"** maps onto a Vietnam system as animated arrows and route lines over period maps: the Ho Chi Minh Trail, troop movements, refugee flows.

### Gaps
- No description was found of the King of the Monsters opening or end titles, including whether the end credits use newspaper clippings.
- No source names the specific newsreel shots in the Kong: Skull Island montage. It is unconfirmed whether it includes Vietnam War or moon-landing footage.
- No runtimes or cut counts for any of these.

## Q3. News-montage prologues: World War Z, Edge of Tomorrow, Dawn and Rise of the Planet of the Apes, Pacific Rim, 28 Days Later (and others)

### Takeaway
The strongest news-montage prologues add a second organising layer on top of the raw news: a steady musical pulse against a sound collage (World War Z), a geographic carrier that turns the news into spread and loss (Dawn's globe, Rise's flight lines), or a narrator plus a timeline that compresses years into minutes (Pacific Rim). They end by draining energy, with lights out, dying notes and the frame converging, more often than by slamming.

### Cited Findings
- **World War Z (2013), Prologue.** The titles use many audio clips that interlock with the images. "The opening Muse score worked like a metronome, subconsciously brightening the tone of the piece", contrasted against "the cacophony of radio announcers and savage imagery". The titles were inspired by *Altered States* and *The Dead Zone*: "mattes and angled lines that track along screen" form the title letters, which "come together at the end" — [Prologue: World War Z](https://prologue.com/portfolio-item/worldwarz/) [search summary; 403 on fetch]
- The Muse track "Isolated System" is described as "not a fist-pumping rock 'n roll anthem but a much more deliberate and ominous instrumental piece". It is on the WWZ soundtrack and was cut into a promo from unseen film footage — [The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/?p=97349) [search summary]; [The Film Stage](https://thefilmstage.com/zombies-attack-in-over-30-new-world-war-z-images-muse-music-video) [search summary]
- **Dawn of the Planet of the Apes (2014), Picturemill.** Picturemill was asked to make a narrative sequence explaining the virus's spread between the two films. Rather than "a conventional editorial news montage", it projection-mapped the news footage onto a globe, extending the design language it had built for the end of *Rise*. It co-wrote the voiceover that drives the sequence, sourced and manipulated the stock footage with 20th Century Fox, and delivered in stereo 3D — [Picturemill on Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/120831063) [search summary; the fetch hit a captcha]
- **Dawn's structure.** Talking-head fragments play over a map of the earth "showing the vectors by which the flu spread". Lights representing civilisation fade out. It ends on the US West Coast, "all dark and empty and cold", with Michael Giacchino's score playing "a few dying notes" — [Alternate Ending review](https://www.alternateending.com/2014/07/ape-ocalypse-now.html/amp) [fetched]
- **Dawn's footage.** The first image on screen is former NYC Mayor Michael Bloomberg. The montage reuses real past warnings from Bloomberg and President Obama about hurricane preparedness, recontextualised as pandemic news — [Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/screens/dawn-of-the-planet-of-the-apes-11922719/) [fetched]
- **Rise of the Planet of the Apes (2011), end credits.** After a short scene where an infected pilot leaves San Francisco for New York, the credits play over a map whose growing lines trace the infection along airline routes, to Europe and then Africa — [MediaStinger](https://mediastinger.com/rise-of-the-planet-of-the-apes-2011-extra-scene-during-the-credits/) [search summary]
- **Pacific Rim (2013), Mirada prologue.** A two-minute, documentary-style sequence with more than 80 VFX shots that compresses roughly five years of kaiju war into backstory. Del Toro "organized reference archival footage around a temp voice over". A physical "war room" wall of concept art, references, storyboards and stills guided it — [CGW](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Web-Exclusives/2013/Pacific-Rim-Prologue-Sequence-Grabs-Attention.aspx) [search summary; 403 on fetch]; [Renderosity/Mirada release](https://www.renderosity.com/amp/article/16718/mirada-teams-with-guillermo-del-toro-for-warner-bros-pictures-and-legendary-pictures-i-pacific-rim-i-prologue-sequence) [search summary]
- **Pacific Rim credits by Imaginary Forces.** A "foreboding war room map collapses inwards, enveloping the emblems of fallen heroes". The film opens with info cards defining "kaiju" and "jaeger" — [Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3242641/video-pacific-rims-opening-and-closing-credits/) [search summary]
- **Edge of Tomorrow (2014).** It opens on a montage of news reports: the Mimics have destroyed and taken over most of Europe and will soon move into England, and newscasters present the UDF's invasion as what will defeat them — [Read the Spirit review](https://readthespirit.com/visual-parables/edge-tomorrow-2014/?print=pdf) [search summary]. The Mimics land in Germany and conquer much of the continent — [Wikipedia: Edge of Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_Tomorrow) [search summary]
- **28 Days Later (2002).** It opens on violent riot footage "culled from television newscasts" — [Scope, Univ. of Nottingham, Aug 2003](https://nottingham.ac.uk/scope/documents/2003/august-2003/film-rev-aug-2003.pdf) [search summary]. The sound of news reports "merges together with screaming and static" and fades toward white noise. A tracking shot pulls back across the bank of screens to reveal a restrained chimpanzee forced to watch — [Zooscope, Univ. of Sheffield](https://zooscope.group.shef.ac.uk/28-days-later-dir-danny-boyle-fox-searchlight-pictures-2002/) [search summary]. The footage is prerecorded and looping, part of the "Rage" experiment — [Ballard104 Omeka: Rage stills](https://ballard104.omeka.net/items/show/35) [search summary]. Sources disagree on how the chimp is restrained.

### Inferences
- **Pulse against chaos (WWZ).** Keep one regular, almost neutral pulse (score as metronome) under an increasingly chaotic audio collage of announcers. Acceleration comes from the density of the collage and the images, not from speeding up the music. This is easy to automate: fixed-BPM bed, with radio and announcer clip density ramping up per phase.
- **The geographic carrier (Dawn, Rise).** Mapping footage onto a globe or map turns news into measurable spread. Lights going out or lines multiplying give a visible quantity that escalates monotonically, which a viewer reads as "it is getting worse" even without understanding the language of the news clips. That matters for Vietnamese viewers watching English-language archive.
- **Recontextualising real clips (Dawn).** Picturemill and Fox reused real hurricane-preparedness warnings as pandemic news. For a documentary this is the line *not* to cross: real archive must stay attached to its real event. The transferable part is pulling generic but authoritative fragments (official podiums, sirens, crowd shots) to build atmosphere around a correctly captioned event.
- **Temp voiceover as spine (Pacific Rim).** Del Toro organised archival references around a temp VO before any picture was final. An automated system should likewise draft the narration or text-card spine first, then retrieve archive per line.
- **Ending patterns.** These prologues end by draining: Dawn goes to darkness and dying notes, 28 Days Later dissolves into white noise and a reveal of the watcher, the Pacific Rim credits collapse inward, and WWZ converges lines into the title. That suits documentaries better than a trailer "slam" (see Q5).

### Gaps
- Not researched within budget: **Contagion** (opening "Day 2" cards and spread montage), **Arrival** (news screens), **Independence Day** (news and TV escalation), **Cloverfield** (found-footage framing card), **28 Weeks Later**. No sourced material was collected for these.
- Edge of Tomorrow: no source on whether the newscasters are real anchors or actors, or on the montage's construction.
- WWZ: one outlet's claim that the film uses 14 minutes of "Isolated System" is unverified. Prologue's page could not be read directly.
- Rise: the designer of the end-credit flight map is not confirmed. Picturemill says Dawn "extended" the design language "created for the ending of RISE", which suggests but does not prove that it made both.

## Q4. Other archival-style title sequences: Watchmen, Chernobyl, Se7en (texture reference); Oppenheimer, Zodiac, X-Files, Stranger Things, Manhunter

### Takeaway
Watchmen shows the "living history" technique: an alternate 1939–1985 told in near-still tableaux with slow and selective motion, with type built into the 3D scene. Chernobyl shows that real recordings and minimal, controlled sound (a dosimeter click ramped in tempo) create more dread than score. Se7en remains the reference for analogue distress, with hand-scratched type, artefacts deliberately kept from the optical printer and a staccato edit.

### Cited Findings
- **Watchmen (2009), yU+co.** It moves through "several stylized eras, starting in 1939 and ending in 1985". "Dynamic slow motion and selective movement" give each shot its look. The edited sequence is about six minutes long. yU+co had to fit titles into a sequence already cut without them: "Rather then simply lay 2D type onto the foreground of the live action, it is incorporated in 3D into each scene." Snyder: "So I literally went to the beginning of the movie and started drawing." The video was taken down at Warner Bros.' request — [Motionographer: yU+Co, The Watchmen Titles](https://motionographer.com/2009/03/06/yuco-the-watchmen-titles/) [fetched]
- **Watchmen credits.** Main titles directed by Zack Snyder; cinematography Larry Fong; title designer/supervisor Garson Yu; title designer/art director Neil Huxley; compositing supervisor Alan Boucek; music Bob Dylan's "The Times They Are a-Changin'". Art of the Title tags it "situational type, type over scene, typographic, CGI, live action, prologue" — [Art of the Title: Watchmen](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/watchmen/) [fetched]
- **Chernobyl (HBO, 2019), editors Jinx Godfrey and Simon Smith.** Smith describes the dosimeter clicks as an instrument he controlled. He first played them live in GarageBand, then found that keyframing tempo in Pro Tools or Logic let him ramp them up and down. He says the dread comes from the characters ("It's the fear that they have") and "We weren't trying to tell a disaster movie." — [ProVideo Coalition, Art of the Cut: Chernobyl](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-chernobyl/) [fetched]
- **Chernobyl, real recordings and restraint.** Smith cites "the real phone call" in Episode 1 and the real evacuation announcement in Episode 2. On the evacuation he says "It was a very restrained use of sound… Just being very minimal with it": viewers hear shuffling and buses, not dialogue. Episode 2 opens on a montage of Soviet mosaic artwork over an untranslated Russian poem reading, which Smith found on YouTube, because it "puts you in the world of these characters and situates them in that place and that time." In the basement scene he used about 20 audio tracks with D-Verb so each drip's echo "would just go on and on". Composer Hildur Guðnadóttir's music is "a kind of hybrid of sound design and score" — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-chernobyl/) [fetched]
- **Se7en (1995), Kyle Cooper, edited by Angus Wall (texture reference).** The type was hand-etched into black scratchboard and manipulated in film transfer "to further smear and jitter it". The transfer was "cut up and reassembled during post production to add a final layer of temporal distress". The team kept accidents from the optical printer ("with fish hooks and razor blades going through the roll-up machine"). Mostly hand assembly gave "an analog warmth and randomness". Notebooks were shot on a tabletop, backlit to show "the shadows behind the page". Wall cut a "staccato edit" to a "nail-on-chalkboard" remix of "Closer". Cooper's aim was to introduce the killer early, because in horror "the monster didn't come out until the third act." — [Art of the Title: Se7en](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/se7en/) [fetched]

### Inferences
- **Watchmen's living photographs.** Near-still, slow-motion "photographs" with one moving element read as history coming alive. For real archive, the honest version is 2.5D parallax or a slow push on real photos, with a little selective motion (smoke, flag) used sparingly and labelled. Watchmen restaged iconic photos with actors, which a documentary must not do.
- **Type inside the scene (Watchmen) vs. type as document (Godzilla).** For war archive, the document approach is more credible. Names, dates and places typed onto period-style paper, cables or maps feel like evidence. Floating 3D type feels like fiction.
- **Chernobyl's clicks.** Its dosimeter-click tempo ramp is a precisely reproducible acceleration device. A diegetic periodic sound (Morse key, teletype, Geiger click, ticking clock, helicopter rotor) with keyframed tempo gives escalation independent of picture cuts. Teletype or Morse fits the era (wire services, military signals).
- **Untranslated period audio as atmosphere (Chernobyl Ep. 2).** Untranslated Vietnamese, French or English radio of the era can carry place and time without subtitles if it is atmosphere, not information.
- **Se7en's lesson for the texture pass.** Distress belongs to the material: scratches, gate weave, jitter, cut-up transfer. A uniform overlay does not achieve it. Grain, weave and scratch should vary by source and era, not one LUT over everything.

### Gaps
- Not researched within budget: **Oppenheimer** trailers, **Zodiac** opening, **The X-Files**, **Stranger Things** and **Manhunter**-style document titles. No sourced findings.
- Chernobyl: the use of real archive photographs and text cards at the end of Episode 5 was not covered by the interview read, so it is not sourced here.
- Watchmen: no source found explaining the Dylan choice or the cut rhythm across eras.

## Q5. Cross-cutting craft: structure, cut rhythm and acceleration, music and sound, text, colour and texture, endings (measurements)

### Takeaway
None of the fetched or summarised sources gives measured shot lengths for these sequences. The sourced craft regularities are structural. A fiction layer (files, maps, globe, voiceover) reorganises real footage. Escalation is carried by a steady pulse plus growing density, or by a visible quantity (lights out, lines spreading, a ramping click). Sound collage dissolves into noise or silence. The ending converges into the title or drains to darkness. Trailer-editing practice adds the "beat of silence, then hard cut to title" convention.

### Cited Findings
- **Trailer structure** (trailer editor Derek Lieu's guide, as summarised): cold open, then Act 1, Act 2, Act 3, then "the button". Act 2 raises stakes "with faster cuts", and the cut rhythm accelerates as the track builds — [Jonny Elwyn: How to Edit a Movie Trailer](https://jonnyelwyn.co.uk/film-and-video-editing/how-to-edit-a-movie-trailer/) [search summary]
- **Silence before the final hit.** The final act ends with a hard cut to the title card, usually preceded by a beat of silence. Dropping to near-silence just before the last hit makes the next impact feel bigger by contrast — [Violet Recording: Sound Design for Trailers](https://violetrecording.com/sound-design-for-trailers/) [search summary]; [Jonny Elwyn](https://jonnyelwyn.co.uk/film-and-video-editing/how-to-edit-a-movie-trailer/) [search summary]. The search summary noted that silence lengths vary by editor, so this is common practice, not a rule.
- **Sound as structure.** WWZ: metronomic score vs. a cacophony of announcers — [Prologue](https://prologue.com/portfolio-item/worldwarz/) [search summary]. 28 Days Later: news audio merges with screaming and static into white noise — [Zooscope](https://zooscope.group.shef.ac.uk/28-days-later-dir-danny-boyle-fox-searchlight-pictures-2002/) [search summary]. Chernobyl: keyframed tempo ramps of diegetic clicks and minimal sound — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-chernobyl/) [fetched]
- **Text devices.** Redaction strike-outs that leave only the names (Godzilla 2014, Godzilla vs. Kong) — [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/comicbook/news/godzilla-opening-credits-what-was-blacked-out/) [fetched]; [IMDb tt5034838](https://imdb.de/title/tt5034838/crazycredits?tab=cz) [search summary]. Matte lines that converge into title letters (WWZ) — [Prologue](https://prologue.com/portfolio-item/worldwarz/) [search summary]. Type built into 3D scenes (Watchmen) — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2009/03/06/yuco-the-watchmen-titles/) [fetched]. Definition cards (Pacific Rim) — [Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3242641/video-pacific-rims-opening-and-closing-credits/) [search summary]
- **Endings found:**
  - Godzilla 2014: a real atomic explosion and shockwave — [fan blog](http://codysfilmandtvblog.blogspot.com/2019/05/franchises-monsterverse-godzilla-2014.html) [search summary]

## Q6. General principles from title designers and editors on building dread with montage

### Takeaway
The sourced principles converge on a few points. Show the threat earlier than the story would (Cooper). Let the material carry the distress (Se7en). Build dread from human fear and restraint rather than disaster spectacle (Chernobyl's editor). Organise archive around a spoken or written spine (del Toro). Imagery should "intrigue without revealing too much" and gain meaning later (Shine on Monarch).

### Cited Findings
- Cooper wanted "to introduce the killer in the titles" because in horror "the monster didn't come out until the third act." The title sequence is used to pull the threat forward — [Art of the Title: Se7en](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/se7en/) [fetched]
- Cooper on keeping accidents: hand assembly gave "an analog warmth and randomness", and "It takes on a life of its own." — [Art of the Title: Se7en](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/se7en/) [fetched]
- Simon Smith on Chernobyl: the dread is "the fear that they have"; "We weren't trying to tell a disaster movie"; he prefers "very restrained" sound — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-chernobyl/) [fetched]
- Shine on Monarch: "Designed to intrigue without revealing too much, the imagery takes on new meaning after watching the first season." — [Shine](https://www.shinestudio.com/project-pages/broadcast/monarch-legacy-of-monsters) [fetched]
- Del Toro organised reference archival footage around a temp voiceover for the Pacific Rim prologue — [CGW](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Web-Exclusives/2013/Pacific-Rim-Prologue-Sequence-Grabs-Attention.aspx) [search summary]
- Prologue's WWZ notes: a steady score subconsciously steers tone while the announcer collage and imagery carry the chaos — [Prologue](https://prologue.com/portfolio-item/worldwarz/) [search summary]
- Picturemill rejected "a conventional editorial news montage" in favour of a globe that carries the news — [Picturemill/Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/120831063) [search summary]

### Inferences
These are rules for the Vietnamese archival war-documentary system (1945–1975), derived from the cases above and not sourced as such:
1. **Every montage gets a carrier layer:** a dossier with redaction, a period map with spreading routes, or a then-and-now split screen. A bare string of newsreel clips is what Picturemill explicitly avoided.
2. **Write the spine first** (narration or text cards), then retrieve archive per line (del Toro).
3. **One steady pulse plus rising density.** Fixed-tempo bed; an era-correct audio collage (radio announcers, Morse or teletype, crowds) thickening per phase. One diegetic periodic sound with a keyframed tempo ramp (Chernobyl).
4. **Document typography with redaction beats.** The reveal, read, strike-out, name rhythm gives on-beat cut points without picture cuts. Only real document text or clearly labelled reconstruction; never fabricated "classified" content presented as real.
5. **Archive stays attached to its real event.** Godzilla and Dawn re-label real footage as fiction, which is fine for fiction and not for a documentary. Use the device (reinterpretation through documents) with true captions.
6. **Show the threat early,** in the first 10–20% (Cooper), but intrigue without revealing (Shine).
7. **End on drain or convergence:** a scale image, a beat of silence (trailer practice), then the title or a fade to black. Ending on dying notes and darkness, as Dawn does, suits tragedy better than a trailer slam.
8. **Texture inherent to source** (Se7en): vary grain, weave and scratch per reel and era. No global filter.

### Gaps
- No primary craft interviews were found specifically on archival dread montages from Prologue (WWZ, Godzilla, Kong), Picturemill (Dawn) or Imaginary Forces (Pacific Rim). Their own project pages were blocked (403 or captcha) or held only short notes.
- No academic or editor-authored source quantifies acceleration in dread montages. Video-essay analyses (YouTube channels) were not located within the tool budget.
