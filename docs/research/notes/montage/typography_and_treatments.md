# Kinetic typography and archival-footage treatments for blockbuster prologues and prestige documentaries, and how to build them headless (browser/Remotion/SVG/CSS + FFmpeg)

Research date: 2026-10-27. Scope: catalogue of text and footage treatments with meaning, named uses, parameters and code recipes. Every row in the "Cited Findings" lists has a URL. Parameter tables and recipes are **my inference** (design judgement plus documented APIs). They have not been rendered or measured against the reference films. Option names for FFmpeg filters were checked against the local build (`ffmpeg 8.1.2-full_build-www.gyan.dev`, `ffmpeg -h filter=<name>`), and the official reference is https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html.

Frame conversions used throughout: 1 frame = 41.7 ms @24 fps, 33.3 ms @30 fps. "f24/f30" below means frames at that rate.

## Q1. Text treatments: what they communicate, where they are used, parameters, legibility, when they look cheap

### Takeaway
Of the requested treatments, the best documented in a named blockbuster is the Godzilla (2014) redaction. Prologue/Kyle Cooper built the main titles as a montage of "classified" documents and 1950s footage in which all legible text is blacked out except each cast/crew name, and the sequence ends on a nuclear whiteout. The other treatments (stamps, typewriter, teletype, slams, tracking-out, glitch, counters, supers, split-flap) are well-established genre grammar, but I found no frame-accurate published specs for them. The parameters below are proposed starting values to tune, not measurements.

### Cited Findings
**Godzilla (2014), the redaction reference**
- Credits: Main Title Designer Kyle Cooper; producer Unjoo Byars; editor/co-producer Andrew Young; design and animation David Badounts, Gary Mau, Matthew Normand, Ana Criado Zahonero; studio Prologue Films; music Alexandre Desplat; 2.35:1. Art of the Title tags it "archive footage, archive photography, editorial, main title, mixed-media, prologue". The page has credits only, with no process write-up. — [Art of the Title: Godzilla (2014)](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/godzilla/)
- The sequence is a montage of Monarch documents and 1950s videos. All text is blacked out except the cast and crew names, and it ends on a nuclear blast that whites out the screen before the title. During Bryan Cranston's credit the only unredacted words are "Walter" and "White" (the Breaking Bad joke). This comes via the search snippet of IMDb's crazy-credits/trivia page. — [IMDb Godzilla (2014) crazy credits](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0831387/crazycredits/)
- Whenever "any large, legible section of text appears on screen" it is "immediately 'redacted'" until only the person's name remains. Credits are "alphabetized by first name". Frame-by-frame viewing after digital release showed much of the redacted text was "silly jokes". — [GNDN blog, 2015](https://gndn.wordpress.com/2015/09/12/godzilla-2014-opening-credits-censored-jokes/)
- Kyle Cooper's earlier acclaimed sequence is Se7en (1995). He also did Godzilla: Final Wars (2004) titles. — [Wikipedia: Kyle Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Cooper); [Vantage Point interview](https://vantagepointinterviews.com/2018/06/17/a-burning-love-for-godzilla-kyle-cooper-on-designing-the-opening-title-sequence-for-godzilla-final-wars/)

**World War Z (2013): title letters built from graphic elements**
- Prologue's main title ends with the title letters assembling from mattes and angled lines. This is from the search-result summary of Prologue's project page; a direct fetch returned HTTP 403. — [Prologue: World War Z](https://prologue.com/portfolio-item/worldwarz/)

**Contagion (2011): day-count supers as structure**
- The film begins on a "Day 2" card and ends with a "Day 1" card. The countdown/count-up super is a structural device, not decoration. — [Wikipedia: Contagion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contagion_(2011_film)); [Deep Focus Review](https://www.deepfocusreview.com/?p=23048); [Screen Daily review](https://www.screendaily.com/contagion/5031510.article)

**Documentary date/place supers (lower thirds)**
- Documentaries commonly add location, date or context captions. An establishing shot plus a place/date caption tells the viewer when and where the scene is. Keep supers inside the title-safe area, about one-third up from the bottom. Rule of thumb: 3–6 s on screen. Use high contrast, a subtle backdrop or shadow over busy footage, one consistent style, and understated graphics. The search engine summarised several guides together, so attribution between them is approximate. — [Soundstripe: A documentarian's guide to lower thirds](https://soundstripe.com/blogs/a-documentarians-guide-to-lower-thirds); [No Film School: What is a lower third](https://nofilmschool.com/what-is-lower-third); [MasterClass: lower third graphics](https://masterclass.com/articles/how-to-use-lower-third-graphics-in-film-and-tv); [Epidemic Sound: lower thirds](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/lower-thirds/)

**Fonts (licence status)**
- **Special Elite** (typewriter face): by Astigmatic (Brian J. Bonislawsky), on Google Fonts since 2011, **Apache License 2.0**, not OFL. Fedora packages it as ASL 2.0. — [Serbyte: Special Elite](https://www.serbyte.net/fonts/special-elite); [RightFont: Special Elite](https://rightfontapp.com/family/special+elite); [Fedora wiki: Special Elite fonts](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Special_Elite_Fonts)
- **Courier Prime**: a redesign of IBM Courier (Howard Kettler, 1956) made for screenplays, licensed **OFL-1.1** per distro package metadata. — [Adélie Linux package: courier-prime](https://pkg.adelielinux.org/current/courier-prime); [MSVSphere package listing](https://repo1.msvsphere-os.ru/msvsphere/9/distroscope/en/packages/q/qu/quoteunquoteapps-courier-prime-fonts-3.018-1.el9.1.src.html)
- **IBM Plex Mono**: SIL OFL 1.1. A 2018 Apache dual licence was rescinded because Apache was considered unsuitable for fonts. — [Wikipedia: IBM Plex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Plex)
- OFL does not require documents made with the font, such as a rendered video, to be OFL. — [IBM Plex Mono licence text mirror](https://maxibestof.one/fonts/coding/ibm-plex-mono-LICENSE.txt)

**Split-flap libraries (open source)**
- `masteryder/splitflap` (vanilla JS, no dependencies, hold-time and tick-speed options); `react-split-flap-display` (React, character set, step speed in ms, optional flip sound); `svelte-split-flap` (Web Audio flip sounds, timing/easing/size props); jQuery Flapper (MIT, legacy). Licences other than Flapper's were not verified. — [masteryder/splitflap](https://github.com/masteryder/splitflap); [react-split-flap-display](https://github.com/tthompson-figma/react-split-flap-display); [svelte-split-flap](https://github.com/socketopp/svelte-split-flap); [jQuery Flapper](https://plugins.jquery.com/flapper)

**Glitch text (code technique)**
- Canonical CSS text glitch: two `::before/::after` copies with `content: attr(data-text)`, offset ±2 px, `text-shadow: -1px 0 red` / `-1px 0 blue`, opaque background to mask the original, and randomly sliced with `clip-path: inset(T% 0 B% 0)` keyframes (~20 steps), animated `2s`/`3s infinite linear alternate-reverse`. A reader comment raises WCAG seizure concerns. — [CSS-Tricks: Glitch effect on text/images/SVG](https://css-tricks.com/glitch-effect-text-images-svg/)

**Typewriter (code technique)**
- `steps(n, jump-term)`: an integer `n` > 0 (> 1 for `jump-none`); default position `end`. `jump-start` starts the output at 1/n; `jump-both` gives n+1 levels; in multi-segment animations the step count applies per segment. — [MDN: steps()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/easing-function/steps)

#### Legibility and "premium vs cheap" (inference)
- **Premium**: one typographic system per film (one typewriter face, one condensed grotesque, one mono), consistent sizes, and long enough holds (≥ 1.5 s for any word the viewer must read). Movement serves meaning: the redaction hides, the stamp judges, the counter escalates.
- **Cheap**: stacking several treatments on one text element (glitch + shake + glow + typewriter); continuous glitch; free "distressed" fonts with fake coffee stains; centred all-caps slams every 10 frames; default bevel or emboss drop-shadows; text on footage without a shadow or scrim.
- **Typewriter faces work best as document props** inside the frame (paper, dossier). For supers and narration cards, a clean grotesque or mono reads as the film's voice. Mixing these two roles is what makes many YouTube docs look homemade.
- **Vietnamese diacritics**: confirm the chosen face covers Vietnamese (e.g. ơ, ư, ạ, ặ). Many typewriter display faces lack full Latin Extended Additional coverage. Coverage of Special Elite and Courier Prime was **not verified** here.

### Gaps
- No published frame-level specs (durations, easing) for the Godzilla 2014 redaction animation, and no Kyle Cooper interview about the 2014 titles was found. Art of the Title has credits only. Bar colour, wipe speed and hold times would need to be measured from the film.
- Contagion's on-screen city/population supers: I recall cards like a city name plus population, but no source confirming the wording or typography was found.
- No authoritative source for typewriter/teletype screen speeds; the 10 cps Teletype figure is from memory.
- Licences of pixel or dot-matrix fonts (e.g. VT323), condensed grotesques (Oswald, Bebas Neue, Barlow Condensed) and Noto were not checked in this session. Check each Google Fonts specimen page before use.
- Vietnamese glyph coverage of the typewriter faces was not verified.

## Q2. Footage treatments: film burn, flash frames, step printing, ramps, punch-ins, gate weave, damage, CRT/VHS, aberration, vignettes, grids, freeze-frames, evidence boards, maps, and what is overused

### Takeaway
Prestige use of footage treatments is motivated and sparse. Dawn of the Planet of the Apes uses a world map whose city lights go out as the connective spine of its news montage. Godzilla turns its archive into "classified" evidence through one consistent rule. Every treatment in the list can be built from documented FFmpeg filters (verified locally) or CSS/SVG compositing. The binding constraint on flash frames and strobing is photosensitive-epilepsy guidance (WCAG 2.3.1 and ITU-R BT.1702), not aesthetics.

### Cited Findings
- **Dawn of the Planet of the Apes (2014)** opens with a montage of news reports. Fragments of talking heads play over a map of the Earth showing how the "simian flu" spread, while lights representing human civilisation fade out. It returns to a dark West Coast, with Michael Giacchino's score playing "somber, dying" notes. — [Alternate Ending review](https://www.alternateending.com/2014/07/ape-ocalypse-now.html) (single-review source)
- The first image is former NYC Mayor Michael Bloomberg, whose disaster-preparedness warnings were repurposed for the outbreak. — [Austin Chronicle review](https://www.austinchronicle.com/screens/dawn-of-the-planet-of-the-apes-11922719/)
- **Godzilla (2014)**: archive footage, archive photography and mixed media, presented as Monarch documents; ends on a nuclear blast whiteout. — [Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/godzilla/); [IMDb crazy credits](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0831387/crazycredits/)
- **Film-look parameters in pro tools**: Dehancer models gate weave with **period, amount and angle** (the period sets how many frames a variation lasts). Grain has size/amount/highlight/midtone/shadow impact, with profiles for 8, 16, 35 and 65 mm. Halation has 8 sliders, and "Impact" works like opacity. Dehancer's own blog says its profiles "may be considered slightly exaggerated". No numeric pixel values were published. — [Dehancer features](https://www.dehancer.com/features); [Dehancer tool profiles](https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/dehancer-tool-profiles/); [Photofocus Dehancer in AE](https://photofocus.com/software/getting-the-film-look-in-after-effects-with-dehancer/)
- **Photosensitivity**: WCAG SC 2.3.1, "Three Flashes or Below Threshold". It uses the BT.1702 general-flash definition (luminance change ≥ 20 cd/m² where the darker image is < 160 cd/m²) and, for HDR, a Michelson-contrast test (≥ 1/17). — [W3C Understanding SC 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG20/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold)
- ITU-R BT.1702 (guidance for reducing photosensitive-epilepsy risk in TV) is in force as **BT.1702-3 (Nov 2023)**. — [ITU-R BT.1702](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.1702-3-202311-I)
- Ofcom Broadcasting Code Rule 2.12 requires precautions for viewers with photosensitive epilepsy. In a 2015 case (The Voice UK), flashing lights shown for more than 5 s in total breached the PSE guidelines. — [hellocatfood: Potentially Harmful (summarising Ofcom)](https://hellocatfood.com/blog/potentially-harmful/); legacy guidance: [Ofcom/ITC gn_flash.pdf](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/16248/gn_flash.pdf)
- **Ubiquity of overlays**: stock libraries sell large film-burn, light-leak and glitch packs. Listings like "Film Light Leaks Transition 11 (Use Blend Mode Add)" show how they are meant to be used (additive/screen). — [Storyblocks film burn search](https://www.storyblocks.com/motion-graphics/search/film-burn); [SpotlightFX free light leaks](https://spotlightfx.com/free/light-leak-transitions)
- **FFmpeg building blocks (options verified on local ffmpeg 8.1.2)** — [FFmpeg filters documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html):
  - `vignette`: `angle` (default `PI/5`), `x0`, `y0`, `mode` forward/backward, `eval` init/frame, `dither`, `aspect`.
  - `noise`: `alls`/`c0s`… strength 0–100; flags `allf=a|p|t|u` (averaged, pattern, **temporal**, uniform); `all_seed`.
  - `rgbashift`: `rh rv gh gv bh bv ah av` from −255 to 255 px; `edge=smear|wrap`; runtime-changeable (T flag). `chromashift`: `cbh cbv crh crv`.
  - `tblend`: blend successive frames; modes include `addition, screen, lighten, difference, burn, dodge, overlay, multiply, glow, heat, freeze…`. `tmix`: `frames` (default 3), `weights` ("1 1 1"), `scale`. `lagfun`: `decay` (default 0.95), a phosphor/trail persistence.
  - `framestep`: `step`. `random`: `frames` (cache 2–512, default 30), `seed` (shuffles frame order).
  - `fade`: `type in|out`, `start_frame`, `nb_frames` (default 25), `color` (default black), `alpha`. `tpad`: `start/stop` frames, `start_mode/stop_mode=add|clone`, `color`; useful for freeze frames.
  - `zoompan`: `z`, `x`, `y`, `d` (default 90), `s` (default hd720), `fps` (default 25).
  - `eq`: `contrast`, `brightness`, `saturation`, `gamma`… as expressions, with `eval=frame` for per-frame evaluation. `curves`: presets including `vintage`, `cross_process`, `strong_contrast`, `negative`; `interp=natural|pchip`. `colorchannelmixer`: rr…aa gains −2 to 2 plus `pc` preserve-colour modes. `geq`: per-pixel expressions for r/g/b/lum/alpha. `deflicker`: `size` 2–129, `mode` am/gm/…/median (for **removing** flicker from real archive). `scroll`: `h`/`v` speed −1 to 1, `hpos`/`vpos`.
  - Most of these support **timeline editing** via `enable='between(t,a,b)'` or frame-based `between(n,a,b)`. Expression variables: `t` (s), `n` (frame index from 0), `w`, `h`. Options marked T can be changed at runtime (`sendcmd`/`zmq`).

#### What is overused or kitsch (inference, consistent with how stock packs are sold)
- **Overused**: light leaks on every cut; film burns as transitions; continuous glitch; red-string evidence boards; "TOP SECRET" stamps on things that were never classified; VHS filters on footage that was shot on film; fake damage over clean modern footage; typewriter sounds under text that isn't typed; radar-ping maps; 16-panel news grids.

- **Rule of thumb**: if removing the treatment loses no meaning, remove it.

## Q3. News montages: making news footage cinematic rather than a "dashboard"

### Takeaway
The verified blockbuster examples (World War Z, Dawn of the Planet of the Apes, Godzilla 2014) treat news and archive as **raw material inside one cinematic idea**. WWZ cuts news against animal swarms and crowds with layered radio audio. Dawn lays news voices over a map whose lights go out. Godzilla uses a redaction conceit. None shows it as on-screen UI chrome. The escalation from local to global is carried by sound and geography, not by boxes and tickers.

### Cited Findings
- **World War Z (2013), Prologue**: a main title built entirely from stock footage of news clips, animal behaviour and crowds, with **radio audio layered over the images**. The director and producers asked for this approach. It ends with the title letters assembling from mattes and angled lines and is scored to a Muse track. Per the search summary, schools of fish and city crowds stand in for the hive-like zombies. (Fetch blocked by 403; details come from the search-engine summary of the page.) — [Prologue: World War Z](https://prologue.com/portfolio-item/worldwarz/)
- A student analysis reads the WWZ opening as building dread through news reporters from different regions and everyday crowds. This is a weak source (student essay). — [SlideShare: horror title sequence analysis](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/horror-title-sequence-analysis/46319876)
- **Dawn of the Planet of the Apes (2014)**: news-report fragments over a world map showing the spread, civilisation's lights fading, then a return to the dark West Coast with Giacchino's dying score. The first image repurposes Michael Bloomberg's real disaster-preparedness warnings. — [Alternate Ending](https://www.alternateending.com/2014/07/ape-ocalypse-now.html); [Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/screens/dawn-of-the-planet-of-the-apes-11922719/)
- In the film's world, the flu progresses from isolated cases to an epidemic to a pandemic; this is the escalation structure. — [Empire: Dawn virals](https://empireonline.com/movies/news/new-dawn-planet-apes-virals-hit-web)
- **Godzilla (2014)**: archival 1950s footage and documents unified by one conceit (Monarch's classified files, redaction), ending in a whiteout. — [IMDb crazy credits](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0831387/crazycredits/); [Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/godzilla/)
- **Contagion (2011)**: day-count title cards (opens on "Day 2", closes on "Day 1") structure the global spread chronologically. — [Wikipedia: Contagion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contagion_(2011_film))

### Inferences
- **Full frame by default.** News footage fills the frame (crop or scale to cover). Station bugs and tickers stay only if they *are* the information (e.g. a foreign-language ticker as texture). A boxed video inside a UI frame reads as a "dashboard"; full frame reads as cinema.
- **One spine.** Pick one connective device and hold it the whole sequence: a map (Dawn), a metaphor intercut (WWZ: swarms, crowds), or a document conceit (Godzilla: redaction). The news clips hang off that spine.
- **Sound carries the montage.** Layer 3–6 voices at once: radio chatter, anchors in several languages, sirens. Duck under one clear line at key beats, and use a musical bed that grows. The cited WWZ radio layering fits this. Silent renders (the project default) lose most of this. Inference: a silent news montage should lean harder on visual escalation and typography (counters, supers) to compensate.
- **Escalation local → global**, as a shot plan (inference):
  1. one local anomaly, a single reporter, a tight shot (2–4 s shots);
  2. several cities, several languages, faster cutting (1–2 s);
  3. institutions (podiums, press conferences, officials) plus the map spreading;
  4. global: aerials, crowds, map fully red or dark, shots of 8–16 f;
  5. hard cut to silence or black, or a whiteout (Godzilla), then the title or day card.
- **Vary sources and texture deliberately**: mix aspect ratios and eras only if each keeps its native look (4:3 kept pillarboxed inside a film-scope frame is acceptable when stylised consistently). Don't apply one filter to every clip. That homogenisation is what makes montages look like templates.
- **Typography is sparse in prestige news montages**: place/date supers on only 1 in 4–6 clips, counters at most 2–3 times per sequence, and no lower-third name straps on anonymous anchors.
- **Ethics and legal (inference)**: repurposing real people's speech (as Dawn did with Bloomberg) is a studio-level clearance decision. For this project, use clearly fictionalised or properly licensed or public-domain clips, and never fabricate a real outlet's broadcast (masthead or logo impersonation).

### Gaps
- No designer interview (Prologue, Dawn's editors) explaining the selection or sound process was retrievable. The Prologue page returned 403, and no Art of the Title feature exists for WWZ or Dawn.
- Shot-by-shot timing (average shot length) of the WWZ and Dawn openings is unmeasured. It would need frame analysis of the films.
- Other likely references (Children of Men's news screens, Watchmen's 2009 archival title sequence, Contagion's montage) were not verified in this session.

## Q4. Implementation: CSS/SVG/canvas techniques, FFmpeg filters, open-source assets

### Takeaway
Everything in the catalogue maps onto a small toolkit:
1. A **frame-driven, seeded** animation layer (Remotion `random(seed)`; never `Math.random()` or CSS time-based animations).
2. A **per-character text scheduler** (typewriter, teletype, coordinates, split-flap).
3. **SVG filters** (`feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` / `feComponentTransfer`) for ink, glitch and tearing.
4. **CSS blend modes and transforms** for leaks, scanlines, shakes and punch-ins.
5. An **FFmpeg finishing pass** for grain, weave, aberration, vignette, step printing, flash frames and fades, with `enable=` timeline gating. All options listed here were verified on local FFmpeg 8.1.2.

### Cited Findings
- Remotion `random(seed)` returns a deterministic 0–1 value for a number or string seed. `Math.random()` triggers an ESLint warning because Remotion renders across multiple threads and page instances and values would mismatch. `random(null)` is intentionally unseeded, and calling it with no argument is an error. Example: `random(\`particle-x-${i}\`)`. — [Remotion docs: random()](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/random)
- SVG `feTurbulence` attributes: `baseFrequency` (one or two values), `numOctaves`, `seed`, `stitchTiles` (stitch/noStitch), `type` (fractalNoise/turbulence). It works in linearRGB by default (`color-interpolation-filters="sRGB"` to change). It is Baseline widely available since July 2015. Example pairing with `feDisplacementMap in="SourceGraphic" in2="turbulence" scale="50" xChannelSelector="R" yChannelSelector="G"`. The defaults (baseFrequency 0, numOctaves 1, seed 0, type turbulence) come from the spec, as reported by the fetch model. — [MDN: feTurbulence](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Element/feTurbulence)
- CSS `steps()` semantics. — [MDN: steps()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/easing-function/steps)
- CSS glitch slices via `clip-path: inset()` with pseudo-element copies. — [CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/glitch-effect-text-images-svg/)
- FFmpeg timeline editing: `enable` expression with `t`, `n`, `w`, `h`. Example `smartblur=enable='between(t,10,3*60)'`, `curves=enable='gte(t,3)':preset=cross_process`. Options flagged T are changeable at runtime. Framesync options for multi-input filters: `eof_action` (repeat/endall/pass), `shortest`, `repeatlast`. — [FFmpeg filters documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)
- An FFmpeg-devel thread notes that frame blending for motion blur was achieved by chaining `tblend` and `framestep=step=2`, limited to power-of-two steps; a `blend` option for framestep was proposed in 2017 (merge status unverified, and the local `framestep` help shows only `step`). — [FFmpeg-devel 2017-May](https://ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-devel/2017-May/211829.html)
- Fonts: Special Elite is Apache-2.0; Courier Prime and IBM Plex Mono are OFL-1.1 (sources in Q1).
- Split-flap reference implementations (sources in Q1).
- Photosensitivity limits for any flash or strobe treatment: WCAG 2.3.1 and ITU-R BT.1702-3 (sources in Q2).

### Inferences

#### Core architecture (inference)
1. **Everything is a pure function of `frame`.** No CSS `@keyframes` with wall-clock time and no `requestAnimationFrame` timing. In Remotion use `useCurrentFrame()` + `interpolate()`. In plain headless HTML capture, expose `seek(frame)`. This matches Remotion's determinism rationale (cited) and the project's HyperFrames seek hooks.
2. **Seeded randomness per element**: `random(\`${id}-shake-${frame}\`)` for white-noise jitter (stamp shake, dust). For smooth motion (gate weave, leaks), sample value noise at `frame/period` and interpolate between seeded lattice points. That keeps it continuous, unlike per-frame white noise.
3. **Per-character scheduler** (one utility powers T3, T4, T12, T14): input text, cps, a jitter seed and pause rules ({",":6, ".":10, "\n":12} frames). Output: an array of reveal frames. The renderer shows char *i* if `frame >= reveal[i]`. Store the schedule in the composition props so audio SFX (typewriter clacks, if sound is enabled) can be placed on the same frames.
4. **Text measurement for redaction**: render, measure word boxes with `Range.getBoundingClientRect()` after fonts load (`document.fonts.ready`), then position the bars. In Remotion, measure in a layout effect guarded by `delayRender`/`continueRender` so capture waits for fonts.
5. **Shake** (stamps and slams): offset(f) = A·e^(−f/τ)·(2·random(seed+f) − 1), with A 6–12 px and τ 2–3 frames. Apply to a wrapper around both footage and text, so the whole "camera" shakes, not the text alone.
6. **Flash QC**: after render, compute mean luma per frame (FFmpeg `signalstats` or Python) and fail the build if more than 3 flash transitions fall in any 1 s window (WCAG 2.3.1, BT.1702). This turns a safety rule into an automated gate.

#### Reusable SVG filter snippets (inference; attributes per MDN)
- **Ink edge / marker bar**: `feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="0.04" numOctaves="2" seed="N"` → `feDisplacementMap in="SourceGraphic" scale="2.5" xChannelSelector="R" yChannelSelector="G"`.
- **Stamp ink dropout**: `feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="1.0" numOctaves="3"` → `feComponentTransfer` (alpha, `type="discrete" tableValues="0 1 1 1 1"`) → `feComposite operator="in" in="SourceGraphic"`.
- **Glitch tear burst**: `feTurbulence baseFrequency="0.0015 0.35" numOctaves="1" seed={frame}` → `feDisplacementMap scale={burst ? 18 : 0}`. Update `seed` only every 2 frames for a "digital" stepping feel.
- **Paper texture**: `feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="0.7" numOctaves="4"` → `feColorMatrix` to low-contrast warm grey → `multiply` onto #EFE9DA.
- Performance note: big SVG filters on 1080p frames are slow in headless Chrome. Cache static textures (paper, stamp masks) as PNG once, and keep live filters for animated elements only.

#### FFmpeg finishing chain example (inference; every option name verified locally, not rendered)
```
ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "\
scale=iw*1.02:-1,\
crop=1920:1080:x='(iw-ow)/2+1.0*sin(n*0.37)+0.6*sin(n*0.11)':y='(ih-oh)/2+0.8*sin(n*0.29)',\
eq=brightness='0.015*sin(2*PI*t*5.3)':eval=frame,\
noise=alls=10:allf=t+u,\
rgbashift=rh=-1:bh=1,\
vignette=angle=PI/6,\
rgbashift=rh=-8:bh=8:enable='between(n,240,242)',\
fade=type=in:start_frame=0:nb_frames=6" -c:v libx264 -crf 16 out.mp4
```
- Step printing on a range: split the stream, apply `fps=12,fps=24` to the segment, and concat. Or pre-quantise source times in the browser renderer (simpler and frame-exact).
- Trails/phosphor: `lagfun=decay=0.85`. Ghost blend: `tmix=frames=3:weights="1 2 1"`. Datamosh-ish difference flicker: `tblend=all_mode=difference` (a look approximation, not real datamosh).
- Archive clean-up before stylising: `deflicker=size=5:mode=pm`, then stabilise (the project already has stabilisation tools).
- B&W/sepia for the "period" look: `colorchannelmixer` (gains −2 to 2), or `hue=s=0` then `curves=preset=vintage`.

#### Open-source/licence-safe assets (inference except where cited)
- **Fonts**: Special Elite (Apache-2.0, typewriter); Courier Prime (OFL-1.1, clean typewriter for dense documents); IBM Plex Mono (OFL-1.1, terminal, coordinates, counters). Condensed grotesques for slams and supers (e.g. Oswald, Barlow Condensed, Bebas Neue) are believed OFL but **unverified here**.
- **Textures** (paper, stamp ink, dust, scratches, light leaks, film burns): generate procedurally (SVG/canvas noise, seeded), so there are no licence questions and every render is deterministic. Free downloadable leak packs found so far are not CC0 (see Q2 Gaps).
- **Split-flap**: port the logic of masteryder/splitflap or react-split-flap-display to frame-driven rendering; check each repo's licence first.
- **Maps**: d3-geo + Natural Earth (public-domain map data is widely stated, but not verified in this session).
