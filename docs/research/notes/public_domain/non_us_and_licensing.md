# Non-US archival footage of Vietnam 1945-1975: rights status and licensing routes

*Research notes as of 2026-10-27. Not legal advice. Several archive sites (British Pathé, Net-Film) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches, so some findings come from search-result snippets of those official pages; these are marked "(snippet)". Statements tagged "(inference)" are my reasoning, not something a source states.*

## 1. Vietnamese sources and Vietnamese copyright law (term for cinematographic works, state-owned works, what is public domain, how licensing works)

### Takeaway
Vietnam protects cinematographic and photographic works for 75 years from first publication (since 1 Jan 2010; it was 50 years before). Works whose term ran out before 2010 stay in the public domain. On that basis, Vietnamese films and photos first published in 1959 or earlier *may* be public domain in Vietnam, but this is unconfirmed. Material from 1960-1975 is almost certainly still protected in Vietnam until 2035-2050, and works from 1948 on are probably protected in the US through URAA restoration. No Vietnamese archive (Viện Phim, Điện ảnh Quân đội/QPVN, VTV, TTXVN, Hãng phim Tài liệu và Khoa học Trung ương) publishes a rate card or a standard licence. Licensing is negotiated case by case, and in 2025 Viện Phim's own director said the legal framework for licensing state-owned film heritage is still unclear.

### Cited Findings
**Law: term of protection**
- 2005 IP Law (Law 50/2005), Art. 27(2)(a): cinematographic, photographic, applied-art and anonymous works were protected **50 years from first publication**. Law 36/2009 amended this to **75 years from first publication** from 1 Jan 2010. If such a work is unpublished within 25 years of fixation, protection is **100 years from fixation**. Protection ends at 24:00 on 31 December of the expiry year (Art. 27(2)(c)). — [Commons: Copyright rules by territory/Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- The 2009 amendment's transitional rule (cited by Commons as Art. 220) kept works in the public domain if their term expired before 1 Jan 2010, and moved works still in term to the 75-year rule. Commons also says the 2005 law was not retroactive. — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- The general term is the author's life + 50 years (Art. 27(2)(b)). — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- Earlier regimes relevant to war-era works: under **Decree 142-HĐBT (1986)**, copyright held by organisations was **unlimited** and passed to the state if the organisation dissolved. Under the **1994 Ordinance**, rights in unknown-author works belonged to the state unless the author was identified within 50 years of publication. — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- Not protected at all (Art. 15): "news of the day as mere press information", legal and administrative documents, official translations. — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- The **PD-Vietnam** tag on Commons says photographs enter the public domain 50 years after first publication. That conflicts with the 75-year term the same page describes, and Commons itself treats the photo term as unclear. **PD-VietnamGov** covers only legal and administrative texts, not images or logos. The page also notes that the government treats works of rival governments (including South Vietnam) from 2 Sep 1945 to 2 Jul 1976 as illegal (PD-South VietnamGov). — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- **US status:** the URAA restoration date for Vietnam is 23 Dec 1998. Vietnamese works protected in Vietnam on that date and published after 1930 were restored in the US. Because the 50-year term was in force on the URAA date, Vietnamese works from before 1 Jan 1948 are public domain in the US. Commons requires a file to be free in both Vietnam and the US. — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- The 2022 amendment (Law 07/2022) added a non-commercial condition to freedom of panorama (Art. 25.1(h)) from 1 Jan 2023. — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)

**Archives in practice**
- **Viện Phim Việt Nam (Vietnam Film Institute)** holds 1,500 feature-film dossiers, 2,030 documentary dossiers and 615 animation dossiers. War films are "đang chiếm một khối lượng rất lớn" (a very large share). Restored titles include "Chiến thắng Điện Biên Phủ". — [SGGP, 14 Jul 2025](https://www.sggp.org.vn/vien-phim-viet-nam-luu-giu-kho-tang-phim-chien-tranh-post803750.html)
- At a Nov 2025 seminar, Director Lê Thị Hà said that state-commissioned films are deposited unlocked, so the Institute can check, look up and research them. Privately produced films are deposited locked and cannot be opened without the copyright owner's permission. She called copyright "điểm mấu chốt" (the crux) for using film heritage and said a framework is needed for usage rights, royalty collection and revenue. Lawyer Lê Quang Vinh called for "cơ chế ủy quyền rõ ràng cho Viện phim để chủ động đàm phán, cấp phép" (a clear authorisation mechanism so the Institute can negotiate and license proactively). The article notes that legal opinions question who may authorise commercial online exploitation of state-owned works. — [SGGP, 25 Nov 2025](https://www.sggp.org.vn/nong-chuyen-ban-quyen-va-khai-thac-di-san-phim-viet-post825318.html)
- (snippet; exact source page unconfirmed) Under **Quyết định 4693/QĐ-BVHTTDL (2013)**, the films Viện Phim releases on its YouTube channel are those "thuộc sở hữu Nhà nước, phim khuyết danh, phim đã hết thời hạn bảo hộ" (state-owned, anonymous, or out of term). — search result set incl. [British Council PDF](https://www.britishcouncil.vn/sites/default/files/mo-va-hoi-tuong-cac-thuc-hanh-xoay-quanh-di-san-phim-viet-nam.pdf) and [Báo Văn hóa](https://baovanhoa.vn/nghe-thuat/phat-huy-gia-tri-tu-lieu-hinh-anh-dong-luu-tru-trong-ky-nguyen-so-183685.html). The PDF timed out on fetch, so this is not verified.
- (snippet) A British Council Vietnam report on film-heritage practice says users of copyrighted material need the rightsholder's consent, "or a notice in mass media" about the use. — [British Council PDF](https://www.britishcouncil.vn/sites/default/files/mo-va-hoi-tuong-cac-thuc-hanh-xoay-quanh-di-san-phim-viet-nam.pdf)
- In 2015 the State Records and Archives Department (Cục Văn thư và Lưu trữ Nhà nước) bought copies and rights to two foreign documentaries and worked with Viện Phim on post-production. No price was published. — [Báo Pháp luật](https://baophapluat.vn/sap-cong-chieu-2-bo-phim-tu-lieu-quy-mua-ban-quyen-nuoc-ngoai-post244827.html)
- In 2025 Truyền hình Quốc hội signed an agreement with Viện Phim to use archive images for commemorative programming. This is an example of the institution-to-institution route. — [Báo Văn hóa](https://baovanhoa.vn/nghe-thuat/vien-phim-viet-nam-khang-dinh-vi-the-trong-chien-luoc-dua-di-san-dien-anh-ra-the-gioi-181765.html)
- National Archives Centre III (Trung tâm Lưu trữ quốc gia III) collects and organises access to state records of national significance from after August 1945. — [Doanh nghiệp Hội nhập](https://doanhnghiephoinhap.vn/khai-mac-trien-lam-gioi-thieu-gan-200-hinh-anh-tai-lieu-luu-tru-tieu-bieu-104838.html)
- TTXVN (Vietnam News Agency) is the government's national news agency. Its press-photo outlet "Ảnh Báo chí TTXVN" is a distribution channel; no licence or price terms were found. — [Thư viện Pháp luật](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/tnpl/3386/Thong-tan-xa-Viet-Nam-TTXVN?tab=0); [list of press-agency channels](https://ketoananpha.vn/danh-sach-tai-khoan-kenh-trang-nhom-cong-dong-cua-co-quan-bao-chi)

### Inferences
- (inference) **Possible public-domain window for 1945-1959 in Vietnam.** If the 50-years-from-publication rule governed state-studio newsreels and photos, then anything first published in 1959 or earlier expired by 31 Dec 2009. The 2009 transitional rule would then keep it in the public domain. Treat this as a hypothesis to check with a Vietnamese IP lawyer. The 1986 Decree's "unlimited" organisational copyright, together with the uncertain transition through the 1994 Ordinance and the 1995 Civil Code, could change the answer. Commons' own conflicting PD-Vietnam tag shows how unsettled this is.
- (inference) **1960-1975 Vietnamese footage is in copyright in Vietnam**: 75 years from publication gives expiry from end-2035 (1960 works) to end-2050 (1975 works).
- (inference) **US exposure:** Vietnamese works first published from 1948 on, and still protected in Vietnam on 23 Dec 1998, were probably restored in the US. They would then be in copyright there regardless of Vietnamese status. This matters for YouTube distribution and Content ID.
- (inference) **Who owns the rights:** for war-era footage made by state studios (Hãng phim Thời sự Tài liệu, now Hãng phim Tài liệu và Khoa học Trung ương; Điện ảnh Quân đội; Giải Phóng studio), the owner is most likely the state, or the successor studio as the organisation that commissioned the work. Viện Phim is a custodian, and its own leadership says it lacks a clear mandate to license. In practice a producer should: (1) identify the producing studio and its current successor; (2) request a written usage agreement from that studio, or from the managing agency (Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism / Cục Điện ảnh for civilian studios; Ministry of Defence for QPVN/Điện ảnh Quân đội); (3) get the physical copy from whoever holds it (Viện Phim, VTV, the studio).
- (inference) Re-uploads of QPVN or Viện Phim films on YouTube are not licences. The current practice of downloading them for research is fine as research, but publication needs either a public-domain basis or a written grant.

### Gaps
- No official licensing page, price list or contact procedure was found for **Điện ảnh Quân đội nhân dân / QPVN**, **VTV archives**, **TTXVN photo/film archive** or **Hãng phim Tài liệu và Khoa học Trung ương**. These need direct enquiry.
- The consolidated 2022 text of Art. 27 and its transitional provisions was not fetched from an official source (e.g. vbpl.vn, WIPO Lex). The 75-year figure here comes via Wikimedia Commons, not the gazette.
- No source was found for the term that applied under the 1994 Ordinance or the 1995 Civil Code to cinematographic works owned by state organisations, which decides whether the 1945-1959 window is real.
- Decision 4693/QĐ-BVHTTDL (2013) was not read directly.
- Ownership articles (IP Law Arts. 39-42 on commissioning organisations and state ownership) were not verified in this session.

## 2. Soviet/Russian and Eastern-bloc footage (Karmen's Dien Bien Phu, Krasnogorsk/Net-Film, DEFA)

### Takeaway
Russian law has a special clause: films made by a legal entity between 1929 and 1956 and first shown in that window are public domain in Russia. That probably covers 1950s Soviet studio newsreels, including Karmen's *Vietnam* (1955). It is not clear-cut, and US status is separately uncertain. In practice, Net-Film (agent for the Krasnogorsk archive) commercially licenses Karmen's film and quotes per minute or second with no public rates. DEFA footage, including the newsreel *Der Augenzeuge* (1946-1980), is licensed exclusively through Progress Film.

### Cited Findings
- **Russian general term:** life + 70 (74) years. The +4 applies to authors who served or worked during the Great Patriotic War; that explanation is from background knowledge, since the page gives only "70 (74)". Anonymous works: 70 years from publication. — [Commons: Copyright rules by territory/Russia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Russia)
- **Film clause (Art. 1281 / implementing law, as summarised by Commons):** works **created by a legal entity between 1 Jan 1929 and 1 Jan 1956** are public domain in Russia if first shown in that period, or not shown at all until 3 Aug 1993. Later films do not qualify. A similar clause covers TASS/ROSTA staff photo and news reports from 1925-1956. — [Commons: Russia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Russia)
- Authors who died before 1 Jan 1952 are PD in Russia. Deaths in 1952-1956 qualify only if the author did not serve in the Great Patriotic War. — [Commons: Russia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Russia)
- **US status of Russian works:** URAA restoration date is 1 Jan 1996. A work published after 1930 and protected in Russia on that date is generally protected in the US. — [Commons: Russia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Russia)
- **Roman Karmen** made *Vietnam* (1955) and restaged key moments of the Dien Bien Phu victory for it. — [Wikipedia: Roman Karmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Karmen). Some genuine Dien Bien Phu footage by French army cameramen (Schoendoerffer's unit), confiscated by the Viet Minh, ended up with Karmen. — [Wikipedia: Dien Bien Phu (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dien_Bien_Phu_(film)). Vietnamese sources say Karmen worked with Vietnamese cameramen in 1954 and on a 1955 film *Việt Nam trên đường thắng lợi*. — [VNA](https://vietnam.vnanet.vn/english/print/quotvietnamquot-by-roman-karmen-17792.html); [Hanoi Times](https://hanoitimes.vn/film-sheds-light-on-indochina-war-19433.html); [QĐND](https://en.qdnd.vn/culture-sports/culture/unforgettable-time-with-director-roman-karmen-in-viet-bac-562800)
- Net-Film lists Karmen's *Vietnam* (1955) as a seven-part documentary, Karmen credited as director, writer and cameraman. — [Net-Film record film-4517](https://net-film.eu/film-4517) (snippet; the page returns 403 to fetchers)
- **Net-Film licensing (snippet):** no published rates. Buyers send a list of clips (link, timecode, licence type), Net-Film quotes per minute and second of footage used, and the fee depends on the type of production. There is a "Request quote" form. — [Net-Film licensing](https://www.netfilm.store/services/licensing/)
- The Krasnogorsk archive (RGAKFD) is the main Soviet documentary film and photo repository. — [PBS Red Files: RGAKFD](https://www.pbs.org/redfiles/rao/archives/rgakfd/index.html); [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/archive/history-in-motion-film-archives-offer-a-look)
- **DEFA:** the DEFA-Stiftung uses rights partners. **Progress Film GmbH** is the exclusive commercial footage service and licenses DEFA material worldwide. Its holdings include about 2,100 DEFA documentaries (1946-1992), the newsreel *Der Augenzeuge* (about 2,000 issues, 1946-1980), and *Blick in die Welt* (1945-1986, via Cinecentrum). Contact: contact@progress.film. The Deutsche Kinemathek handles cinema-screening licences only. — [DEFA-Stiftung services](https://defa-stiftung.de/en/films/services); [Progress archive](https://summit.progress.film/history)

### Inferences
- (inference) Karmen's *Vietnam* (1955) was a studio production (Central Studio of Documentary Film, TsSDF) shown in 1955, so it plausibly falls under the 1929-1956 legal-entity film clause and is **public domain in Russia**. Karmen's own life+70 term (he died in 1978, which would run to 2048) would not apply if the legal-entity clause governs. Unconfirmed: the production entity, the co-production status with Vietnamese studios, and the first-showing date all need checking.
- (inference) **US status of 1950s Soviet films is uncertain.** Whether *Vietnam* (1955) was PD in Russia on 1 Jan 1996 depends on whether the legal-entity clause applied then (the 1993 law used life+50). If it was protected on that date, it was restored in the US. Treat it as potentially copyrighted in the US.
- (inference) Soviet newsreels **from 1956 onward** (e.g. Vietnam War coverage of 1965-1975 in *Novosti dnya* or *Soyuzkinozhurnal*) are not covered by the clause and are likely in copyright in Russia (life+70 of the authors) and the US. Use Net-Film or a direct RGAKFD licence for them.
- (inference) Net-Film's quote-based pricing means budgeting by benchmark. Western archive rate cards (see section 4) suggest hundreds to a few thousand GBP/USD per minute depending on rights. Net-Film's actual rates are unknown.
- (inference) Even if Karmen's footage is PD in Russia, the physical master and any Net-Film download carry contract terms. A PD route needs a copy obtained without such terms (e.g. an old broadcast or a Vietnamese archive copy).

### Gaps
- No Net-Film or RGAKFD rate card or standard contract text could be retrieved (403).
- No source was found that confirms Karmen's *Vietnam* (1955) is PD in Russia, or that settles its US status.
- German term rules for DEFA newsreels were not verified this session: whether newsreel footage counts as a film work (life+70 of the principal director, screenwriter and others) or as "Laufbilder" (shorter neighbouring right). Progress Film's Vietnam holdings and rates were also not confirmed.
- Other Eastern-bloc sources (Czech Krátký film, Polish WFDiF/PKF, Cuban ICAIC/Santiago Álvarez's Vietnam films, Chinese CCTV/August First) were not researched.

## 3. French sources (ECPAD, INA)

### Takeaway
French Indochina War film (1945-1954) is mainly held by **ECPAD**, the Ministry of Armed Forces image agency. Its holdings are viewable at imagesdefense.gouv.fr, but commercial reuse is licensed under general terms of sale (ecpad.fr/cgv) on a per-quote basis. **INA** licenses broadcast archive through its professional platform (inamediapro). No open licence for either archive's Indochina film was found, and neither publishes current documentary rates in sources reached here.

### Cited Findings
- ECPAD holds audiovisual and photo archives spanning more than a century, about **15 million photos and 94,000 hours of film**, and makes its digitised archives available to the public at **www.imagesdefense.gouv.fr**. — [ECPAD job listing 2026](https://choisirleservicepublic.gouv.fr/offre-emploi/2026-2210851/)
- A user complaint about an **August 2025 ECPAD quote** says the customer had to tick acceptance of the general terms of sale published at **ecpad.fr/cgv**. The terms ran to 12 pages that had to be initialled and signed, and the user called the quote expensive. This is a single anecdote. — [plus.transformation.gouv.fr](https://www.plus.transformation.gouv.fr/experiences/6603694_pire-que-du-juridisme)
- French Army image production in Indochina is documented in ECPAD and the Service historique de la défense (SHD). — [Contextes (2024) via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/f97e5e2c6fb3457b8ffc78c073774eaf)
- **INA**: a paid professional catalogue via **inamediapro.com**. A 2011 study counted 500,000 digitised hours of TV and 300,000 of radio offered to professionals. INA holds exploitation rights over programmes produced by public broadcasters and their extracts, once one year has passed since first broadcast. — [Ithaka S+R case study: INA 2011](https://sr.ithaka.org/publications/linstitut-national-de-laudiovisuel-2011/)
- An example INA agreement (2013, a municipal deal, not a general tariff) priced archive rights at a fixed sum, capped extracts at **30 seconds each**, allowed only the stated purpose, and reserved all other rights to INA. — [Aix-Marseille-Provence deliberation annex](https://deliberations.ampmetropole.fr/documents/ct1/deliberations/2013/10/25/ANNEXE/A085C.pdf)
- For comparison, Gallica (BnF) treats any revenue-generating reuse, including audiovisual production, as commercial reuse under a separate tariff and licence. Third-party copyrighted items cannot be reused without the rightsholder's permission. — [Gallica PDF](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5110114w.pdf)

### Inferences
- (inference) ECPAD Indochina footage was shot by military cameramen (Service cinématographique des armées) in 1945-1954. Under French law, audiovisual works run for life+70 of the co-authors, so most of it is probably **still in copyright** or at least controlled by ECPAD as owner. In practice: browse imagesdefense.gouv.fr, then request a quote per clip and use under the CGV.
- (inference) The real Dien Bien Phu combat footage (Schoendoerffer's unit) that reached Karmen may have French (ECPAD) as well as Soviet claimants. This is a chain-of-title risk worth flagging to an E&O insurer.
- (inference) INA covers French TV news on the 1965-1975 American war and the 1973 Paris accords. Expect short-extract licences with per-extract caps and fixed fees.

### Gaps
- The text of ECPAD's CGV and its tariff grid were not retrieved. Whether any ECPAD images are released under France's open licence (Licence Ouverte/Etalab) was not found. Searches found no evidence of open-licensed ECPAD Indochina film.
- No current INA documentary price per second or minute was found.
- The legal basis on which ECPAD claims authorship rights in conscript or military cameramen's work was not verified.

## 4. British and commercial newsreels and resellers (British Pathé, Reuters/British Movietone, AP Archive, ITN, Getty/CriticalPast/FootageFarm)

### Takeaway
None of these collections is free for publication. British Pathé states its collection is in copyright and that any project, commercial or not, needs a licence. AP and British Movietone put footage on YouTube for discovery, but reuse still requires a licence. The only public price benchmarks found are the BFI's rate card: £840 for the first minute (single-country TV, 5 years) up to £3,600 for the first minute (worldwide online, perpetual). Resellers of US public-domain footage (CriticalPast and others) cannot claim new copyright in exact copies under US case law (Bridgeman), but their download terms are contracts and their Content ID registrations still cause claims.

### Cited Findings
- **British Pathé** (snippet of its FAQ/licensing pages): the collection "is still under copyright and not in the public domain". A licence fee is needed for any commercial **or non-commercial** project, and YouTube viewing does not change that. Online licences are priced by platform, territory and duration. Bespoke or offline deals are priced **per second with a 60-second minimum**. A "Digital Content Creator Programme" exists for eligible creators; terms not retrieved. — [British Pathé FAQ](https://britishpathe.com/faq); [Licensing](https://www.britishpathe.com/licensing/); [Copyright](https://www.britishpathe.com/copyright)
- One filmmaker reported paying about **$2,619.73 per minute** for perpetual, online-only, worldwide rights (older, single source). — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/editorial/how-much-too-much-stock-video-and-photography-9438)
- **BFI rate card (benchmark):** £840 for the first 60 s plus £14/s after, for single-country TV up to 5 years. Worldwide online in perpetuity is **£3,600 for the first 60 s plus £60/s**. Documentaries get a lower all-media perpetual rate. Prices exclude VAT and may change. — [BFI archive footage licensing rates](https://www.bfi.org.uk/archive-content-sales-licensing/archive-footage-sales/archive-footage-licensing-rates)
- **AP Archive and British Movietone:** in 2015 they uploaded over a million minutes (about 550,000 clips) to YouTube channels, with British Movietone covering 1895-1986. The aim was discovery and **promoting licensing deals**. Viewing is free; reuse in productions needs a licence. Licensed programme makers can download masters within minutes. — [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/news/ap-uploaded-lot-history-youtube); [Inverse](https://inverse.com/article/4754-the-associated-press-is-loading-550-000-archival-news-clips-onto-youtube); [CREATe talk](https://www.create.ac.uk/creative-reuse/searching-for-sherlock/)
- **Resellers' claims over public-domain material:** in *Bridgeman v. Corel* (SDNY 1999), a photograph that is an exact copy of a public-domain 2D work lacks originality. The ruling binds only that district and concerns slavish copies of 2D works. Contract restrictions in sale or download agreements generally cannot be enforced against third parties who never agreed to them. — [Roy Rosenzweig Center: Digital History, copyright ch. 6](https://chnm.gmu.edu/digitalhistory/copyright/6.php.html); [Wikipedia: NPG/Wikimedia dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Portrait_Gallery_and_Wikimedia_Foundation_copyright_dispute)
- A law-review article reports that some institutions make image users agree that reproduction against licence terms "will be an infringement of copyright". — [Hastings Comm/Ent L.J. vol. 30(2)](https://repository.uclawsf.edu/hastings_comm_ent_law_journal/vol30/iss2/2)

### Inferences
- (inference) British Pathé's and Reuters' Vietnam-era newsreels are UK productions that the archives themselves treat as in copyright (Pathé says so explicitly). Budget a per-second licence or replace the footage. The exact UK term rules for 1950s-70s newsreels were not checked.
- (inference) For **US-government footage resold by CriticalPast, FootageFarm or Getty**: the underlying film is PD-USGov. Exact digital transfers almost certainly add no new US copyright (Bridgeman reasoning applied to faithful transfers). If you obtained the file from the reseller you are bound by its licence as a contract, and their Content ID fingerprints may still trigger claims. Safer route: source the same reel from NARA or another .gov or Internet Archive copy, and keep the provenance (NARA identifier) in the licence ledger.
- (inference) For budgeting a 10-episode series using about 2-3 minutes per episode of commercial archive, the BFI benchmarks put worldwide-online-perpetual rights at roughly £3,600-£10,800+ per episode before negotiation. This is an order-of-magnitude estimate only.

### Gaps
- **ITN Source / Frontline by ITN, and Reuters' current archive platform:** no findings this session. The current owner and the representative of British Paramount, Gaumont British, Reuters and ITN archives need verifying; British Pathé is reported to represent the Reuters historical collection, but this was not verified.
- **AP Archive** current rate card or YouTube-reuse terms: not found.
- **Getty Images, CriticalPast and FootageFarm** licence text and how each describes its PD material: not retrieved. No litigation specific to these resellers was found.
- Whether Pathé or Movietone offer any free-for-education tier that would cover a public YouTube documentary: not found. Assume not.

## 5. Open repositories: Wikimedia Commons, Europeana and others (Vietnam-era moving images under PD or CC)

### Takeaway
Wikimedia Commons has only a small set of Vietnam War videos, almost all US-military or amateur, and it requires each file to be free in **both** its source country and the US. That rules out most Vietnamese, Soviet and French footage from 1948 on. The practical open-licence pool for 1945-1975 Vietnam motion pictures is US-government material. Non-US PD candidates are narrow: possibly pre-1956 Soviet studio films and possibly pre-1960 Vietnamese publications.

### Cited Findings
- **Category:Videos of the Vietnam War** on Commons contains **9 files** directly plus 2 subcategories (Cu Chi Tunnels, 7 files; William Foulke Collection, 17 files). Examples: *Activities of the 1st Infantry Division, South Vietnam 1968*; *Operation San Angelo, 1st Brigade, 101st Airborne Division 1968*; *CH-47 Chinook in Vietnam War, A Day in the Life of a Helicopter Pilot (1967), U.S. Army*; three Republic of Korea Army clips (1967-1969); Super 8 amateur film of Phu Cat Air Base (1966-67); *1965-02-08 Showdown in Vietnam*. Licence templates must be checked on each file page. — [Commons: Category:Videos of the Vietnam War](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Videos_of_the_Vietnam_War)
- Commons requires works from Vietnam and from Russia to be PD or freely licensed in both the source country and the US. — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam); [Commons: Russia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Russia)
- PD-Vietnam tag status is internally inconsistent (50 vs 75 years); PD-VietnamGov does not cover images. — [Commons: Vietnam](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Vietnam)
- Under PD-Russia logic: films by legal entities first shown 1929-1956 are PD in Russia, as are TASS/ROSTA staff photo and news reports from 1925-1956 under similar conditions. — [Commons: Russia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Russia)

### Inferences
- (inference) Commons is a thin source of moving images for this series. Its value is mainly (a) US-military films that can be traced back to NARA originals, and (b) Commons' rules pages as a quick rights test per country.
- (inference) A Vietnamese photo on Commons tagged PD-Vietnam should not be relied on for publication without checking its publication date against the 75-year rule and the US 1948 cutoff, because the tag's own wording is disputed.
- (inference) If the 1945-1959 Vietnamese PD window and the 1929-1956 Soviet clause both hold, 1954 Dien Bien Phu material (Karmen; Vietnamese newsreels of 1954-55) is the most promising non-US candidate. US status remains the blocker for YouTube distribution.

## 6. Fair use / fair dealing, Vietnam's exceptions, YouTube Content ID, E&O insurance

### Takeaway
In the US, the 2005 Documentary Filmmakers' Statement of Best Practices in Fair Use led E&O insurers to cover documentaries that rely on fair use for archival material used for commentary or critique. Vietnam's IP law has an explicit exception for quoting a work "without distorting the author's idea" in a **documentary film** or for commentary. That exception requires crediting author and source and not harming normal exploitation. It does not cover wholesale use of footage as illustration. Content ID does not apply legal tests: PD and fair-use material is routinely claimed, so documentation matters.

### Cited Findings
- **Vietnam IP Law 2005, Art. 25(1)(b):** "Trích dẫn hợp lý tác phẩm mà không làm sai ý tác giả để bình luận hoặc minh họa trong tác phẩm của mình" (reasonable quotation, without distorting the author's idea, for commentary or illustration in one's own work). **Art. 25(1)(c):** quoting a work without distorting the author's idea "để viết báo, dùng trong ấn phẩm định kỳ, trong chương trình phát thanh, truyền hình, **phim tài liệu**" (for journalism, periodicals, radio, TV, documentary films). **Art. 25(2):** users must "không được làm ảnh hưởng đến việc khai thác bình thường tác phẩm, không gây phương hại đến các quyền của tác giả, chủ sở hữu quyền tác giả; phải thông tin về tên tác giả và nguồn gốc, xuất xứ của tác phẩm" (must not affect normal exploitation or prejudice the author's or owner's rights, and must give the author's name and the source). **Art. 25(3):** the exception does not apply to architectural works, fine-art works (tác phẩm tạo hình) or computer programs. The page reflects the 2005 original text. — [Hệ thống Pháp luật: Điều 25 Luật SHTT 2005](https://hethongphapluat.com/luat-so-huu-tri-tue-2005/dieu-25)
- The 2022 amendment renumbered these provisions. Art. 25 is now "Các trường hợp ngoại lệ không xâm phạm quyền tác giả" (exceptions that do not infringe copyright) and Art. 26 is "Giới hạn quyền tác giả" (limitations on copyright). — [Kế toán An Pha: Luật SHTT 2022](https://ketoananpha.vn/luat-so-huu-tri-tue-nam-2022); [Luật Dương Gia](https://luatduonggia.vn/?p=602975)
- Vietnamese commentary on what counts as "trích dẫn hợp lý" (reasonable quotation). — [Luật sư Việt Nam (lsvn.vn)](https://lsvn.vn/hieu-nhu-the-nao-la-trich-dan-hop-ly-tac-pham1637987724-a112279.html)
- **US documentary fair use:** the *Documentary Filmmakers' Statement of Best Practices in Fair Use* (2005) was drafted by filmmaker organisations with lawyers and academics. Before it, filmmakers generally could not get E&O insurance unless every clip was cleared. Afterwards, insurers (Media/Professional, then others) began covering fair-use claims for films that followed the Statement, with Stanford's Fair Use Project offering pro bono counsel. — [EFF: Film insurers recognize fair use (2007)](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2007/02/film-insurers-recognize-fair-use); [IDA: Fair Use Put to Good Use](https://documentary.org/node/34824); [Current.org](https://current.org/?p=14782)
- The executive producer of *Hoop Dreams* and *Life Itself* said *Life Itself* and several other documentaries would not have been possible without relying on fair use, given archival clearance costs (paraphrase). — [Project DisCo](https://www.project-disco.org/?p=7449)
- **YouTube Content ID:** NBCUniversal, Al Jazeera, Discovery and others claimed public-domain FedFlix government videos through Content ID. The dispute grounds offered (misidentification, permission, fair use) did not fit exact PD copies, and the system penalised uploaders but not false claimants. — [Guardian report relayed on NCUC list (2011)](https://lists.ncuc.org/pipermail/ncuc-discuss/2011-December/013833.html); [Nexa list](https://server-nexa.polito.it/hyperkitty/list/nexa@server-nexa.polito.it/thread/4427FKVLAN2P6KSZCTOZBO4YDMGWVLVH/). NASA's own archive video was taken down after a National Geographic claim during the Demo-2 broadcast and was restored after escalation. — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/?p=368347)
- YouTube's current dispute flow: a dispute can be filed on grounds of having the necessary rights, fair use, or misidentification. The claimant has 30 days to respond, and abusive disputes can bring penalties. — [YouTube Help: Dispute a Content ID claim](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797454)

### Inferences
- (inference) Vietnam's Art. 25(1)(c) "quotation in a documentary film" is narrower than US fair use. It is quotation of a work, implying a short, attributed excerpt that serves the documentary's argument, and it must not substitute for licensing (the "normal exploitation" test). Using minutes of QPVN or Karmen footage as visual wallpaper probably falls outside it. A short, credited clip that is analysed on screen is the strongest case.
- (inference) Distribution on YouTube engages US law (fair use) and the uploader's local law (Vietnam) at the same time. Footage that is only "fair use in the US" may still infringe in Vietnam, and vice versa.
- (inference) An E&O insurer will expect a clearance log: source, rights basis (licence / PD with reasoning / fair-use or quotation opinion letter) and chain of title per clip. The repo's licence ledger (`editvideo/ledger.py`) is the natural place to store this. A fair-use reliance usually needs a written opinion from clearance counsel.
- (inference) Even fully PD or licensed footage will draw Content ID claims from archives that registered their copies (AP, Pathé, resellers). Keep licence documents and PD provenance ready for disputes, and expect monetisation holds during disputes.
